I am a recent graduate in Computer Science and Engineering (Major: Information Systems) from the American International University–Bangladesh (AIUB), where I completed my B.Sc. with a CGPA of 3.89/4.0. My research focuses on machine learning and explainable AI for climate extremes and heat-health risk, with an emphasis on combining ground-station time series with remote sensing and spatial data for spatiotemporal prediction. I work with deep learning architectures such as GRU and LSTM, gradient-boosted models like XGBoost, and explainability methods including SHAP and LIME.

My first-author paper, *"Enhancing Heatwave Prediction Using Explainable AI: Feature Selection and Model Optimization for Improved Forecasting,"* was accepted at COMSYS 2025, presented at the conference, and is to appear in the proceedings (Springer Nature). I worked as a Research Assistant under Dr. Nuzhat Tabassum at AIUB (May 2025 – March 2026), and we are now preparing a manuscript on a three-phase pipeline — feature-based prediction, time-series forecasting, and explainability — applied to 14 years of daily temperature data for Dhaka and extended to Rajshahi, Chittagong, and Sylhet. I am currently a Research Student at Elite Research Lab, where I receive structured mentorship in manuscript writing and work in mentor-led research groups, currently co-authoring a team paper. In parallel, I am independently developing a machine learning framework for next-day heat risk prediction (Safe, Caution, Danger, Extreme Danger) across eight cities in Bangladesh, using Heat Index–derived risk labels and SHAP to identify city-specific risk factors toward an early-warning framework.

I am currently applying to graduate programs (Master's/PhD) to pursue rigorous, long-term research on climate-resilient AI systems — building models that are not only accurate but interpretable enough to inform real-world early-warning systems, particularly for climate-vulnerable regions like South Asia.
